Data & references

Every map on this site is built from public, open-source data. This page describes what those datasets are, where they come from, and how they are licensed, and then lists the literature the analysis and narrative rest on. The full version — version strings, file paths, record counts, access dates, and known issues — is in docs/data-sources.md.

Two principles govern the data on this project. First, Phase 1 uses open data only — no partner or restricted data is used or published. Second, no precise puma location is ever published (see the note at the foot of this page).

The data

Where animals were recorded

  • GBIF (Global Biodiversity Information Facility) — the main occurrence source, pulled as a citable, DOI-stamped download, so the exact record set is reproducible. Records carry a coordinate-uncertainty value that the analysis respects when snapping points to units.
  • iNaturalist — community wildlife observations. iNaturalist obscures many sensitive species by default, but not mountain lions in California, which is exactly why the sensitive-data handling on this project is strict.
  • GBIF target-group background — for the bobcat occupancy model, a second, much larger GBIF pull of all vertebrate records across the region stands in for “where did people look at all,” which is what lets the model separate effort from presence.

Where habitat is protected

  • CPAD (California Protected Areas Database) — parks, preserves, watershed lands and other fee-owned open space, with the unit hierarchy the occupancy site definition needs.
  • CCED (California Conservation Easement Database) — conservation easements on otherwise private land, held as a separate layer and combined with CPAD for total protected coverage.
  • TIGER/Line county boundaries (US Census, via tigris) — the ten-county study frame.

What the landscape is made of?

  • ESA WorldCover 2021 — 10 m land cover (trees, shrub, built-up, cropland, and so on), the base habitat layer. A known trade-off: WorldCover under-maps California chaparral badly compared with older US products, which is why urban-intensity comes from human modification instead.
  • Global Human Modification (gHM) v3, 2022 (Theobald et al. 2024) — a 0–1 index of how heavily each area is modified by people; the dominant term in the puma resistance surface.
  • SILVIS housing density — block-level housing as a second human-footprint source.
  • AWS Terrain Tiles (via elevatr) — elevation, from which slope is derived. The tiles blend several public elevation sources at roughly 30 m effective resolution here.

Roads and traffic

  • OpenStreetMap roads (Geofabrik NorCal extract) — the road network, classified by type. Because roads are a barrier only when busy, road presence alone is not enough.
  • Caltrans AADT — annual average daily traffic counts, which turn “a road is here” into “how much traffic crosses here.” A structural caveat: traffic count stations cluster on busy roads, so any spatial fill of the gaps has a built-in upward bias, handled with a source flag.

Road mortality (pending)

  • CROS (California Roadkill Observation System) / UC Davis Road Ecology Center — the intended wildlife–vehicle collision source. Record-level access is still pending, so the collision-risk question is currently answered as a risk-overlap proxy, not as a mortality point layer.

Population context

There is no repeated regional census of Bay Area pumas or bobcats — nothing like the national tiger census that anchors the sister project. A single statewide puma abundance estimate exists (the California Mountain Lion Project, roughly 3,200–4,500 statewide) and is cited only as one-time context. No statewide bobcat abundance estimate exists at all.

NoteOn the puma listing

The Santa Cruz Mountains population — the one anchoring this project’s connectivity story — falls within the listed DPS. The listing is recent and post-dates the project’s model training; it was verified against the primary Commission notice. Because the formal effective date follows the vote, this site cites the February 12, 2026 vote, not an effective date, and points to the primary Commission notice for the adopted findings.

Sensitive data

ImportantNo precise puma locations are published

iNaturalist does not obscure mountain lions in California, so this project holds precise puma records that could expose real animals if published as points. Published puma outputs are therefore always generalised — continuous surfaces at a 1 km grain, or summaries at the open-space-unit level — never point maps, camera locations, den sites, or kill sites. Bobcat data, lower-sensitivity, is published at finer resolution but reviewed under the same process. The full policy is in docs/sensitive-data-policy.md.
